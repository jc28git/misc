\textbf{Notation.} $L$ = labor, $K$ = capital, $Q$ = output, $W$ = wage (price of labor), $R$ = rental rate (price of capital).
\[
\MPL = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta L}, \qquad \MPK = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta K}.
\]
The marginal product of labor ($\MPL$) is the extra output from one more unit of labor, holding capital fixed. The marginal product of capital ($\MPK$) is the extra output from one more unit of capital, holding labor fixed.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*, itemsep=6pt]

\item Define the marginal rate of technical substitution of labor for capital, $\MRTS_{LK}$. What does it measure, and how is it related to the isoquant?
\answerspace{2.6cm}


\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Use the following.} The marginal rate of technical substitution equals the ratio of marginal products, $\MRTS_{LK} = \dfrac{\MPL}{\MPK}$. A firm minimizes the cost of producing a given output where the isoquant is tangent to the lowest isocost line:
\[
\underbrace{-\frac{W}{R}}_{\text{slope of isocost}} = \underbrace{-\frac{\MPL}{\MPK}}_{\text{slope of isoquant}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{\MPL}{W} = \frac{\MPK}{R}.
\]

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*, itemsep=6pt, resume]

\item Suppose that at the firm's current input mix, $\dfrac{\MPK}{R} > \dfrac{\MPL}{W}$. Interpret this inequality. Is the firm minimizing cost? Should it use more capital or more labor? Explain.
\answerspace{3.3cm}


\item Suppose instead that $\dfrac{\MPL}{W} > \dfrac{\MPK}{R}$. Interpret this inequality. Should the firm use more capital or more labor? Explain.
\answerspace{3.3cm}


\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Problem 2 (7.16)}

Suppose a firm has the following production function: $Q = 2KL$. The marginal product of capital for this production function is $2L$, and the marginal product of labor is $2K$. If capital rents for \$100 per unit per day, labor can be hired for \$200 per unit per day, and the firm is minimizing costs,

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}, itemsep=8pt]
\item Find the total cost of producing 60 units of output.
\answerspace{3cm}


\item Suppose you want to find the cost of producing a more general $q$ units of output. Using the same set of procedures you followed in (a), but using a more general $q$ instead of 60, find this firm's total cost function, which expresses total cost as a function of $q$.
\answerspace{3cm}


\item Divide the total cost function you found in (b) by $q$ to find this firm's average cost function.
\answerspace{2cm}

\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Problem 3 (8.8)}

Hack's Berries faces a short-run total cost of production given by
\[
TC = Q^3 - 12Q^2 + 100Q + 1{,}000,
\]
where $Q$ is the number of crates of berries produced per day. Hack's marginal cost of producing berries is $MC = 3Q^2 - 24Q + 100$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}, itemsep=8pt]
\item What is the level of Hack's fixed cost?
\answerspace{1.5cm}


\item What is Hack's short-run average variable cost of producing berries?
\answerspace{2cm}


\item If berries sell for \$60 per crate, how many berries should Hack produce? How do you know? (\emph{Hint:} You may want to remember the relationship between $MC$ and $AVC$ when $AVC$ is at its minimum.)
\answerspace{4cm}


\item If the price of berries is \$73 per crate, how many berries should Hack produce? Explain.
\answerspace{4cm}

\end{enumerate}
